CarMirror

Your Android phone's apps on the Tesla screen. Direct over your hotspot, low lag, and built to keep playing while you drive.

Get the app Source on GitHub

What it does

Any Android appYouTube for the passenger, navigation, music, your own apps. Pick which ones show up in the car.
FastVideo goes straight from the phone to the car over the hotspot, decoded in hardware. No cloud relay in the way.
Touch worksTap, swipe, type: the car screen controls the phone. The keyboard shows up on the car screen.
Made for drivingThe picture is drawn on a canvas, not a video player, which is what the browser blocks while the car moves.
Car-sized appsWith Shizuku running, each app gets its own screen at the Tesla's exact size, and split view for two apps side by side.
Focus modeHides the phone's status bar, navigation bar and home screen, so only the app shows.

What you need

  • An Android phone (Android 11 or newer) with mobile data and a hotspot.
  • A Tesla with the in-car browser. Tested on a 2023 Model Y; newer Teslas with the AMD computer work best.
  • Optional: Shizuku for car-sized app screens and split view.

Setup on the phone (once, about 5 minutes)

  1. Install the app from the download page. Your browser will ask to allow installing apps from it.
  2. Open CarMirror and follow "1 · Phone setup":
    • Touch from the car: Settings → Accessibility → Installed apps → CarMirror → on. If the switch is greyed out: App info → ⋮ (top right) → Allow restricted settings, then try again. (Android asks this for apps not installed from the Play Store.)
    • Open apps from the car: allow "Display over other apps".
  3. Pick your apps in "3 · Apps shown in the car".
  4. Optional, for car-sized screens: install Shizuku and start it with Wireless debugging (needs Wi-Fi, once after every phone restart). Then tap Allow in CarMirror.
  5. Tip: on Samsung, a Modes & Routines rule "when connected to the car's Bluetooth → turn on hotspot" makes every drive hands-free.

In the car

  1. Turn on the phone's hotspot and connect the car to it (car Wi-Fi settings).
  2. Open the car's browser at carmirror.outsmartis.dev.
  3. The first time, type the 6-digit code shown in the phone app. The car stays paired after that.
  4. Tap an app. Without Shizuku, the phone asks once per drive to share its screen: tap Start now.

In the car: Hide bars gives the app the whole screen, Fullscreen hides the browser's own bars, and Settings has video quality, frame rate and focus mode. Sound plays from the phone, so connect the phone to the car over Bluetooth.

CarMirror picks the mode by itself: car-sized screens when Shizuku is running, the phone screen otherwise.

How it works

Phone: the app encodes the picture as H.264 in hardware → WebRTC sends it directly over the hotspot (the server only introduces the car and the phone) → Tesla browser: WebCodecs decodes in hardware and draws on a canvas → your taps go back the same way.

Nothing you watch goes through the server. It only handles pairing and helps the two find each other.

Good to know

  • Apps that block screenshots (banking, Netflix and similar) show black. That's the app's choice and can't be worked around.
  • The car must be on the phone's hotspot. Over the car's own LTE, the phone can't be reached directly.
  • Video uses no mobile data: it stays between the phone and the car.
  • Watching video while driving is the passenger's job. Follow your local laws.
  • This is a hobby project, built in a few days and offered as is.

Something not working?

  • Open carmirror.outsmartis.dev/diag in the car: it checks what the browser supports.
  • In the phone app, "Last problem" shows the most recent error.
  • Report issues on GitHub.
